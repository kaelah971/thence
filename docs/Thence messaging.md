# Thence — Messaging

**Product:** Thence  
**Pronunciation:** `thens`  
**Descriptor:** Recipient-first cross-asset payments inside Nimiq Pay  
**Competition category:** Pay — Nimiq Mini Apps Competition Cycle III  
**Status:** Brand locked; product-language and messaging system  
**Scope note:** This document intentionally excludes visual identity direction, including logo, colour, typography, imagery, layout, motion and art direction.

> **Pay from what you have. They receive what they use.**

This document is derived from the Thence seven-things scope and the supplied product-idea and PRD/build-plan PDFs. It is an editable messaging replacement, not a claim that every target capability is already shipped.

## 1. Strategic message

### What Thence is

Thence is a Nimiq Pay Mini App for paying someone in the asset and network they use, even when the sender starts with NIM.

### What Thence solves

The sender should not have to become a trader, bridge operator or gas manager to complete a normal payment.

### What Thence promises

The sender specifies what the recipient should receive. Thence checks a supported route, keeps Nimiq Pay as the approval boundary and presents a clear status and receipt.

### What makes it different

Thence begins with the recipient and exact receive amount, not with a swap pair or chain route. It hides route machinery without hiding approval, fees, availability or proof.

## 2. Positioning

Unlike a swap dashboard or manual bridge flow, Thence begins with the beneficiary and exact receive amount, keeps Nimiq Pay as the approval boundary, hides route mechanics and exposes proof when delivery is verifiable.

## 3. Audience messages

### For NIM holders

You should not have to buy another asset just to pay someone. Choose the recipient, enter what they should receive and approve the NIM payment in Nimiq Pay.

### For recipients

Specify the asset and network you accept. Send a request so the payer sees the right destination and amount before approving.

### For Nimiq judges and builders

Thence makes Nimiq Pay essential to a real payment experience: NIM is the source balance, native Nimiq Pay approval is the funding boundary and the recipient can receive a supported destination asset without adopting NIM.

### For technical reviewers

Thence separates payment intent, quote, approval, provider progress, destination evidence and receipt state. The backend stores metadata, not user funds or money-moving secrets.

## 4. Messaging house

### Roof: core message

> **Pay from what you have. They receive what they use.**

### Pillar 1 — Start with the recipient

**Message:** A payment begins with who is being paid and what they should receive.  
**Proof:** Recipient book, exact-receive Send flow, saved destination preference and Request to Pay.

### Pillar 2 — Keep the route in the background

**Message:** Thence handles supported route complexity without making the user manage swaps and bridges.  
**Proof:** Route registry, live quote, network guard, fee/minimum disclosure and native approval handoff.

### Pillar 3 — Make payment state explainable

**Message:** The user should know whether a payment is ready, moving, delivered or needs attention.  
**Proof:** Durable statuses, reconciliation, reload recovery and proof-backed receipts.

### Foundation

- NIM is the sender’s real source balance.
- Nimiq Pay approves NIM movement.
- Unsupported routes are not presented as available.
- An unknown outcome is not automatically called failed.
- Delivered requires destination evidence.

## 14. Objection handling

### “Why not just swap NIM first?”

You can, but then you become responsible for the asset, network, gas and route choices. Thence lets you define the recipient outcome first and keeps the funding step inside Nimiq Pay.

### “Does Thence hold my funds?”

The target architecture is non-custodial: Nimiq Pay approves NIM movement, and the backend stores payment metadata rather than user funds or private keys. Verify the current implementation before making a public production claim.

### “Is the estimate a balance I own?”

No. The home-card destination figure is an estimate of what the current NIM balance could send as. The exact NIM debit is shown by Nimiq Pay before approval.

### “Is every network supported?”

No. Thence should show only routes that are currently enabled and quoteable. Availability, limits and fees can vary by route.

### “What if the app closes after I approve?”

The target flow persists recovery-critical state before handoff and resumes or reconciles the transfer after reload. Until that behaviour is exercised, describe it as a target rather than a guarantee.

### “What does delivered mean?”

Delivered means the current route has destination evidence sufficient to support that state. Provider acknowledgement alone is not the same as destination delivery.

## 18. Competition messaging

### Submission description draft — within the official 250-word limit

> Thence is a Nimiq Pay Mini App for paying someone in the asset and network they use, even when the sender starts with NIM. The sender chooses a recipient or opens a payment request, enters the exact amount the recipient should receive, reviews a supported route and approves the NIM payment in Nimiq Pay. Thence hides swap and cross-network mechanics behind a bank-style payment lifecycle: ready to pay, converting, sending, checking and delivered. Recipients can save their preferred asset, network and destination so repeat payments do not require technical re-entry. A receipt brings together the sender-side payment and destination-side proof when available. The product is non-custodial by design: the target architecture keeps funds and money-moving secrets out of the backend, while native Nimiq Pay approval remains the user’s control boundary. Thence belongs in the Pay category because it extends where NIM can go without requiring the recipient to adopt NIM. The MVP should prove one exact-receive route end to end, show safe unknown-state handling and distinguish quote-level evidence from completed settlement.

This draft must be updated after implementation evidence is available. Do not claim live delivery, production readiness or route support that has not been exercised.

## 20. Messaging tests

Before submission, test the following with a stranger who has not read the PRD:

1. Can they say what Thence is after five seconds?
2. Can they identify who the product is for?
3. Can they explain what the recipient receives?
4. Do they understand that the sender starts with NIM?
5. Do they understand that an estimate is not an owned balance?
6. Do they know what to do when the state says Checking payment?
7. Can they repeat the primary line accurately?
8. Do they mistake Thence for a trading app or bridge?
